# Thesis

## The statement
For most households, borrowing tools from a shared library beats owning them: it costs less,
wastes less, and leaves you with more neighbors you can call.

## Why it matters, and to whom
For anyone with a garage full of things they used once, and for neighborhoods where people
no longer know who lives three doors down.

## What would prove it wrong
If members quit faster than they join because the tool they needed was out, or if the money
saved is smaller than the time lost to pickup and return.

## The strongest opposing view
Ownership is a form of readiness. The value of a tool is not the hours you use it; it is
having it the moment the pipe bursts. A library cannot sell you that, and pretending
otherwise is a hobbyist's argument dressed up as economics.

## Options considered and why this one won
Option 2 (loneliness) became chapter 4. Option 3 (city funding) became the epilogue.
The author chose option 1 and agreed to write the availability chapter honestly.
